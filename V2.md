# V2 — ideas parked deliberately

Things worth building that are **not** in v1, with enough reasoning that the
next person (or a future us) doesn't have to re-derive the decision. See
`CLAUDE.md` for what v1 actually is.

---

## The strategic frame that applies to all of these

**The corpus is the moat; generation is not.** A refreshed, structured corpus of
Canadian listings — skills, seniority, comp, eligibility gates, posting age,
cross-board dedup — compounds and is hard to copy. Resume and cover-letter
generation is the most commoditized capability in this space: Teal, Rezi,
Jobscan, Kickresume and plain ChatGPT all do it, none of them need a corpus,
and none of them are differentiated by it.

So the test for any idea below is: **does the corpus make our version better
than a general-purpose LLM tool's version?** If not, it's a feature we might
ship for completeness, not a reason anyone chooses us.

## 1. Modular resumes (tailored per application)

**The idea.** Hold every skill, role, bullet and project the user has as
structured content, then assemble a resume tailored to a specific posting —
reordering and re-emphasizing rather than rewriting from scratch.

**Why it's tempting.** The pain is real and universal; tailoring by hand is
tedious, and most people therefore don't do it.

**Where our version could actually differ.** Not the tailoring itself — the
*target*. Competitors tailor to one job description. We could tailor to what
comparable Canadian postings actually ask for: "across this employer's 40 open
roles they consistently emphasize X", or "for backend roles in Toronto at your
level, Kubernetes appears in 34% of postings and you have it buried on page
two". That is corpus-derived and not reproducible without the corpus.

**Hard constraints if we build it.**

- **Reorder and re-emphasize true content only. Never invent, never inflate.**
  This is an ethical line and also a practical one: a fluent resume that
  overstates gets the user caught in the interview, which is a worse outcome
  than not being shortlisted.
- Keyword-stuffing for ATS is an arms race we should refuse to enter. Optimize
  for a human reader; if that also helps ATS, fine.
- It is a product surface, not a feature: document layout, PDF/DOCX export,
  versioning, an editing UI, and a diff view so the user can see what changed.

**Good news on sequencing.** P3 already builds most of the substrate — resume
upload → native-PDF extraction → structured, user-reviewed profile *is* the
skills-and-experience inventory this needs. What's genuinely new is the
tailoring logic and the render/export layer.

## 2. Cover letters in the user's tone of voice

**The idea.** Generate a cover letter for a specific posting, matched to the
user's own writing voice from samples they provide.

**Assessment.** Cheapest of these to build (text, no layout) and the
tone-matching from the user's own writing is a real differentiator over generic
output. But the value is **unvalidated**: many Canadian tech postings don't ask
for a cover letter at all, and a detectably-LLM letter can hurt. Validate demand
before investing — ask users, or measure how many of our own corpus postings
even request one (we have the descriptions; it's a one-hour query).

**Constraint.** Same honesty rule as resumes: the letter argues for real
experience, it doesn't manufacture enthusiasm or claims.

## 3. Ghost-job / staleness intelligence (standalone)

Measured 2026-08-17: median Canadian board posting is **42 days old**, 26% are
older than 90 days, only 11% are under a week, and board turnover varies wildly
by employer (Cohere's median posting 81 days, Benevity's 17).

In v1 this becomes an *input* to matching and a freshness filter. The parked
larger version: a first-class "is this posting even alive?" signal —
repost history via `content_hash` + `external_id` over time, disappearance
detection, per-employer board turnover rates, "reposted 3× in 6 months".
LinkedIn actively obscures this; we accumulate it for free by polling every 6
hours. Could stand alone as a product for people who don't want to upload a
resume at all.

---

## 4. Junior-friendly employer intelligence

Track which Canadian employers actually hire ≤2-years candidates, and how fast
their boards move. Output is a **curated target list**, not another feed — for a
new grad, "these 14 companies actually hired juniors this quarter" beats 7,000
listings. Aggregates the same extracted `min_years_experience` the v1 matcher
uses, so it's mostly a reporting layer over data we'll already have.

## 5. Things considered and rejected (don't re-propose without new evidence)

- **LangChain / any LLM framework.** Extraction is one schema-constrained call
  per listing; `messages.parse()` with a Pydantic model does it natively. The
  things we actually need — exact per-call token accounting for the cost cap,
  Batch API for backfills, retry-with-error-appended, recorded-fixture tests —
  are all *harder* through a framework wrapper, and framework churn is a
  maintenance cost under a live product.
- **RAG over the user's resume.** A resume is ~600–1,000 words and fits whole in
  context. Chunking it severs a role from the skills belonging to it and drops
  sections that didn't keyword-match but were the deciding evidence. Embeddings
  over *jobs* (the corpus) are correct and are the v1 plan; embeddings over one
  resume are overhead.
- **Visa/sponsorship as the flagship.** See CLAUDE.md's product thesis for the
  measured hit rates (<1% actionable). Still extracted, just not the wedge.
- **Self-managed VPS to cut costs.** Infrastructure is ~$10–25/mo against an LLM
  line of $60–150/mo at scale; migrating optimizes the smaller number while
  taking on backups, patching, TLS and uptime — and P3 puts PIPEDA-relevant
  resumes in that database. Revisit only on a stated trigger (see CLAUDE.md
  stack decisions).
- **Arq + Redis workers.** Deferred, not rejected: at ~31 new listings/day it
  buys nothing for $10–15/mo. The distributed-lock gap it closes only matters
  when running more than one instance.
